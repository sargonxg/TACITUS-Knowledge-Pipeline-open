# Critical review and revision record · v1.1

Publication: WT-2026-01, 24 November 2026. This is an author/publisher revision of a working paper, not independent peer review. No new generation, survey collection, or experimental result was added.

## Scientific issues addressed

1. **Target quantity and evidence.** The weighted 2024 GSS distribution is now consistently described as a survey estimate under its instrument, universe, and period. Agreement with it is distinct from population truth. Population masses and uncertainty in human estimates have different roles.
2. **Bundled intervention.** National and twelve-cell generation change demographic prompting, model-call structure, and aggregation together. Their contrast cannot identify the causal effect of weighting. The manuscript no longer generalizes the observed negative comparison into a claim that weighting generally harms generation.
3. **Earlier-human baseline.** The 2022 distribution is explicitly a temporal-persistence baseline on recurring questions. Its advantage here does not establish that humans universally outperform models or that a new representative survey was run alongside generation.
4. **Practical research question.** The title and a new workflow distinguish questionnaire preparation and assumption exploration from representative polling. An explicitly authored bus-service example illustrates how a researcher might inspect, revise, and then test a question. It supplies no deployment result.
5. **Separate evaluations.** The prospective agenda separates distribution agreement from the usefulness of instrument preparation. It specifies controlled ablations, retained failures, independent expert assessment, human comprehension checks, and complete time/cost accounting. These experiments are proposals, not completed tests.
6. **Provenance boundary.** A small graph links inputs, revisions, executions, findings, and comparisons. The capsule relationship is conceptual; this paper does not invent a complete ontology engine or claim that Wind Tunnel implements the TACITUS App interchange. Public replay, reconstruction, and hosted generation replay are distinguished.
7. **Scoring interpretation.** The existing half-scaled Brier score is retained and explained as expected categorical loss. Its reference-matched loss is generally nonzero; the excess is half the squared distance. Three mathematical displays remain native MathML in HTML.
8. **Legacy scope.** Authored archetypes, signal indicators, the curated showcase, and seeded perturbations move to a separate appendix. The GSS comparison does not validate these legacy outputs.
9. **Presentation.** Four real table captions, two vector figures, restrained typography, versioned citation/downloads, and an archived v1 link improve scholarly reading. The figure distinguishes the human-survey reference from generated cases. No DOI, journal acceptance, or external review is claimed.
